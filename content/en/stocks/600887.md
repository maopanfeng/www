**Inner Mongolia Yili Industrial Group Co., Ltd. (SSE: 600887)**

[中文版](/cn/stocks/600887/)

**Information cutoff: November 1, 2026. Prepared and published on November 2, 2026.** Financial sources are the 2025 annual report, disclosed April 30, 2026; the 2026 interim report, disclosed August 27; and the third-quarter report, disclosed October 30. The interim and quarterly statements are unaudited. The valuation uses the October 30 closing price, the last trading day before the cutoff, rather than November 2 prices. Unless stated otherwise, monetary amounts below are **RMB 100 million**; rounding may cause small differences.

This research distinguishes **reported facts, author calculations and investment assumptions**. Yili has substantial brand and distribution assets, but recovering revenue, recovering earnings and good capital allocation have not yet converged. At approximately 19.2 times trailing attributable earnings, leadership and dividends alone do not establish undervaluation.

## The long-term investment hypothesis

### 1. From dairy scale to cash returns per share

Liquid dairy, milk powder and dairy products, and ice cream should be assessed separately. Ambient milk relies on procurement, manufacturing and national distribution. Infant nutrition depends on trust, formulation and specialist retail channels. Ice cream depends on weather, seasonal demand and freezer coverage. Regional fresh-milk businesses operate within shorter transport radii; advantages in ambient milk do not automatically carry over.

**Reported facts:** the following are category revenues and gross margins, not operating margins after selling expenses. [S1, printed pages 17–18; S2, note VII.61]

| Category | 2025 revenue | Year-on-year | Gross margin | H1 2026 revenue | 9M 2026 revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Liquid dairy | 704.22 | -6.11% | 31.43% | 365.90 | 557.21 |
| Milk powder and dairy products | 327.69 | +10.42% | 41.58% | 168.45 | 248.23 |
| Ice cream | 98.22 | +12.63% | 37.89% | 90.73 | 101.18 |

Nine-month category figures come from the October 30 operating-data announcement, S10. Q3 liquid dairy, powder and dairy products, and ice cream revenue were 191.31, 79.78 and 10.45 respectively; sequential comparisons across different seasons do not establish a trend.

First-half ice cream revenue was already close to the previous full-year figure, illustrating seasonality; doubling interim revenue would be inappropriate. Milk powder and dairy products include nutrition for different ages, cheese and dairy ingredients, rather than only infant formula. Consolidated revenue includes other activities and is not the sum of these three rows.

**Investment assumption:** procurement, distribution and brands create shareholder value only if repeat consumer demand supports margins without excessive promotions, inventory or incremental investment. The questions are whether liquid dairy can stabilize volume and pricing, whether nutrition growth produces profits after channel spending, and whether overseas investments increase capital returns.

### 2. Competition and the durability of the moat

National ambient dairy competes with large groups including Mengniu. Infant formula competes with domestic and international suppliers such as Feihe, Danone and Nestlé. Fresh and chilled dairy faces New Hope Dairy, Bright Dairy and local brands. These are qualitative business comparisons, not market-share rankings.

**Reported facts and calculations:** distributor revenue was 1,103.08 in 2025, approximately **96.3% of principal-business revenue**, and 607.83 in H1 2026, approximately **95.3%**. [S1, printed page 18; S2, note VII.61] For 9M 2026, distributor revenue was 879.56, approximately 94.9% of principal-business revenue by calculation. Distributor count was 17,248, down 531 year-on-year. [S10] The announcement does not sufficiently explain that decline; consolidation or operating changes could contribute, so it establishes neither channel deterioration nor improved efficiency. Distributor shipments are not consumer purchases. Channel loading, rebates and retail discounts can separate factory sales from genuine consumption.

The interim report cites Nielsen and Syntun data putting combined infant cow- and goat-milk formula retail value share at 18.6%, up 0.6 percentage points. This is **third-party research quoted by the company**. The underlying retail sample was not independently obtained, and the figure is not a measure of the entire dairy market. [S2, management discussion]

**Assessment:** brands reduce perceived consumer risk, production scale can lower unit costs, and dense distribution supports replenishment and shelf access. These benefits require continuing marketing expenditure. Discounting, e-commerce, instant retail and retailer bargaining power can erode them. Durable advantage should appear in returns after marketing costs, rather than only advertising visibility or revenue rankings.

### 3. Industry supply is different from consumer demand

On October 19, the National Bureau of Statistics reported that national milk output increased 2.4% in the first nine months of 2026. Agriculture Ministry monitoring released October 14 put raw-milk prices in ten major producing provinces at RMB 3.10/kg for the first week of October, sampled October 8, up 2.0% year-on-year. The production and sampled-price series cover different populations. Neither is Yili's procurement cost or a measure of national dairy consumption. [S5, S6]

**Assessment:** these observations do not establish that excess raw-milk supply has been fully eliminated. Higher milk prices may help farms while pressuring processors. Low prices may help procurement margins while coinciding with weak retail demand and aggressive promotions. Track raw-milk prices, consumer sell-through and processing margins together.

Sources: S1, principal financial indicators and consolidated cash-flow statement; S2, corresponding statements; S3, pages 1 and 9–10. The revenue measure consistently excludes the separately reported interest revenue included in total operating revenue.

The quarterly report directly discloses Q3 revenue of 292.51, up 2.40%; attributable earnings of 30.45, down 5.60%; and attributable earnings excluding reported nonrecurring items of 29.36, down 4.86%. Nine-month attributable and adjusted earnings declined 15.56% and 15.55%. Smaller quarterly declines are encouraging, but do not yet establish renewed earnings growth. [S3, page 1]

**Facts and calculations:** nine-month asset impairment losses were 25.41 versus 3.65 a year earlier; income-tax expense was 23.61 versus 14.60. Dividing tax expense by pretax profit gives effective rates of approximately 21.46% and 12.29%. The interim report identifies 4.50 of adjustments to prior-period income tax and 15.47 of Ausnutria goodwill impairment recognized in Yili's consolidated accounts. [S3, pages 7–8; S2, notes VII.27 and VII.77]

Ausnutria's impairment including the notional minority-interest portion, 26.14, must not be confused with the 15.47 recognized in Yili's consolidated statements. Neither impairment nor tax adjustments can simply be added to attributable earnings without considering taxation and ownership. Earnings excluding reported nonrecurring items do **not** remove every impairment or tax effect. Goodwill impairment is noncash today, but records disappointing returns on past capital allocation.

### 5. Cash flow and working capital

The simplified cash measure here is **OCF minus cash paid for fixed assets, intangible assets and other long-term assets**. The resulting 113.07 for 2025 and 133.23 for 9M 2026 are author calculations, not company-defined free cash flow or cash necessarily distributable to common shareholders. The measure does not fully deduct acquisitions, lease principal or funding requirements of financial activities, and does not distinguish maintenance from expansion spending. The company does not separately disclose those two capex components.

The quarterly report attributes stronger OCF to higher sales receipts and increased amounts payable to suppliers, reducing purchase payments. Payment timing therefore contributes to the improvement. [S3, page 3]

| Consolidated balance | December 2025 | September 2026 | Calculated change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Trade receivables | 29.87 | 36.96 | +7.09 |
| Inventories, net carrying amount | 108.23 | 86.76 | -21.47 |
| Notes payable | 16.51 | 76.14 | +59.63 |
| Trade payables | 114.79 | 125.71 | +10.91 |
| Contract liabilities | 105.64 | 51.08 | -54.56 |

Source: S3, pages 5–6. Lower inventory can reflect better turnover, impairments or seasonality. Lower contract liabilities indicate less customer advance funding, but year-end Lunar New Year preparations make comparison with September imperfect. Notes and trade payables together increased approximately 70.55; that balance change cannot be equated directly with incremental OCF.

For a reproducible **partial working-capital measure**, define W as receivables plus inventory minus notes payable, trade payables and contract liabilities. W moved from -98.84 to -129.20, a decline of approximately 30.36. This excludes prepayments, taxes and other accounts and is not a complete cash-flow reconciliation. Future checks should compare the same quarter across years, bill maturities, distributor inventories and returns rather than assume permanent funding benefits.

Cash investment in long-term assets declined from 22.23 in 9M 2025 to 21.18 in 9M 2026, approximately 4.7% by calculation. Lower investment helps near-term cash flow, but maintenance of equipment, capacity and quality must still be funded. [S3, page 10]

### 6. Deposits are not all immediately available cash

September 2026 assets were 1,570.52 and liabilities 965.10, implying a **61.45% liability-to-asset ratio**. Monetary funds were 172.82, but cash and cash equivalents were only 141.87. Short-term borrowing was 598.77, long-term borrowing 6.08, current portions of noncurrent liabilities 1.81, and noncurrent lease liabilities 2.79. [S3, pages 5–6 and 10]

Those four financing-liability lines total **609.45**, an identifiable subtotal rather than an exhaustive financing-debt estimate. The quarterly report lacks full notes, so financing embedded in other current liabilities cannot be assumed to be zero. Subtracting cash equivalents yields 467.58, a conservative funding-gap observation **before deducting term deposits**, not final net debt for enterprise valuation.

The interim report links increases in other current and noncurrent assets to certificates of deposit and term deposits. At December 2025, the annual report separately identified approximately 135.54, 108.73 and 281.09 of these instruments within other current assets, current portions of noncurrent assets, and other noncurrent assets: approximately 525.35 in total. These amounts sit outside monetary funds and must not be double-counted or treated as immediately accessible without checking maturity and restrictions. [S1, notes VII.12, VII.13 and VII.30; S2, balance-sheet analysis]

**Assessment:** cash alone understates available financial resources, while total deposits alone can understate refinancing risk. Match deposit restrictions, maturities and yields against borrowing and commercial-paper rollovers. Supply-chain lending, factoring and guarantees introduce credit exposure; their receivables are not cash and their risks are not eliminated by dairy brand strength. [S1, note VII.13]

### 7. ROIC: make the definition explicit

Reported 2025 weighted-average ROE was 20.87%; this is not ROIC. The following **broad ROIC proxy** retains financial assets and should not be interpreted as the return of the dairy operations alone.

- **Assumed numerator:** NOPAT = (pretax profit + interest expense) × (1 − 25%). The standardized 25% tax assumption is not Yili's actual effective rate. For 2025, (129.94 + 13.66) × 75% = 107.70.
- **Calculated denominator:** consolidated total equity plus short- and long-term borrowing, current portions of noncurrent liabilities, noncurrent lease liabilities and short-term bonds payable, minus cash equivalents. Average December 2024 and December 2025 balances, including minority equity and goodwill.
- **Inputs:** equity/financing liabilities/cash equivalents were 570.08/605.58/245.47 at December 2024 and 585.44/598.50/176.78 at December 2025. Average invested capital was approximately 968.68, yielding a proxy ROIC of **11.1%**. [S1, consolidated statements and notes VII.43–44]

Pretax profit includes deposit interest, investment and nonoperating results; the denominator retains term deposits and financial assets. Treating all current portions of noncurrent liabilities as financing is also an approximation. Excluding financial assets requires removing their income as well. Excluding goodwill or adding back impairment can obscure acquisition costs. This proxy should not be ranked against differently defined ROIC figures or replaced with a mechanically annualized nine-month number.

## Management and capital allocation

### 8. Integrity, operating ability and stewardship are separate questions

**Governance evidence:** the 2025 audit opinion was unqualified. The interim report disclosed no nonoperating related-party fund occupation or irregular guarantees, marked the relevant reporting-period violation/penalty section not applicable, and reported no unfulfilled effective court judgments or material overdue unpaid debt. These are bounded disclosures and an audit opinion, not proof of management character or every historical action. [S1, important notices; S2, significant matters]

The annual report states there is no controlling shareholder or actual controller. A long-serving team alongside dispersed ownership makes board oversight, remuneration, employee share plans and related-party terms important. Management ownership does not automatically ensure complete alignment. [S1, printed page 67]

**Operating ability:** nationwide distribution and multiple categories are positive foundations. Revenue recovery alongside declining adjusted profit still requires scrutiny of sell-through, spending, taxation and asset quality. Management-defined core-profit measures need reconciliation to statutory earnings before being used to judge performance.

**Capital allocation:** renewed Ausnutria impairment challenges acquisition returns. New projects and international expansion should be judged on incremental cash returns and invested capital. Simultaneous large deposits and borrowing also require scrutiny of maturity matching and net financing returns.

The annual report's 2025 dividend proposal combined a final RMB 0.90 per share with an already paid interim RMB 0.48: RMB 1.38 for the financial year, approximately 87.29 in total, or 75.48% of attributable profit. Financial-year attribution differs from payments during a calendar year. [S1, important notices] The simplified 113.07 cash measure covers that total approximately 1.30 times, by calculation, before other financing, leasing and investment needs.

The October 30, 2026 interim dividend **proposal** is RMB 0.42 per share, approximately 26.56 in total or 30.17% of nine-month attributable profit. Shareholder approval is still required. It is neither an already paid dividend nor a full-year distribution. [S4]

The 2026 buyback plan authorizes 10–20 of own funds, a ceiling of RMB 39.53 per share and cancellation of purchased shares. The quarterly report confirms shareholder approval on September 16 and an initial purchase of 1,800,200 shares on October 22. Planned spending is different from executed spending; purchase is different from cancellation; the price ceiling is not a promised intrinsic value. [S7; S3, page 4] Assess actual purchase costs and completed cancellations against a defensible value estimate.

## Price and expectations

### 9. Valuation and explicit scenarios

The October 30 close was **RMB 30.15**, observed through Longbridge and cross-checked against Investing.com historical prices. These are third-party market data rather than financial-report disclosures. [S8, S9] Multiplying by the reported 6,325,360,667 shares gives approximately **1,907.10** of equity market value. Shares bought back but not cancelled have not been removed in advance.

**Author calculations:** trailing attributable profit = FY2025 115.65 + 9M2026 88.04 − 9M2025 104.26 = 99.43. This gives **19.18 times trailing earnings**, versus 16.49 times FY2025 profit. The financial-year dividend of RMB 1.38 divided by RMB 30.15 gives a historical yield of approximately **4.58%**. This is not a forecast, and the RMB 0.42 proposal must not be added to misstate the same financial year's payout.

The following is a **hypothetical equity cash-flow model**, not an annualization of nine-month OCF. Assume year-zero sustainable common-shareholder cash flow of 100, equivalent to RMB 10 billion; growth of 0%, 3% or 5% for years 1–5; perpetual growth of 2% from year 6; and equity discount rates of 8%, 10% or 12%. The initial cash flow is neither guidance nor an established normalized figure. It is assumed to be after maintenance and expansion investment, taxes, net interest, leasing, working capital and reasonable financing repayment, and attributable to common shareholders. No extra net cash is added and financing proceeds are not treated as distributable cash.

| Five-year growth assumption | 8% discount rate | 10% discount rate | 12% discount rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0% | 1,556 | 1,171 | 939 |
| 3% | 1,776 | 1,330 | 1,063 |
| 5% | 1,936 | 1,446 | 1,152 |

Values are equity values in RMB 100 million. Formula: sum for t=1…5 of 100×(1+g)^t/(1+r)^t, plus 100×(1+g)^5×1.02/(r−2%)/(1+r)^5. A 20% change in initial cash flow changes every value by 20%. At 3% growth and a 10% discount rate, value is approximately 1,330, or RMB 21.03 per share. Supporting the 1,907 market value under those assumptions requires initial sustainable shareholder cash flow of approximately **143.4**, above the 2025 simplified measure of 113.07, even though those two measures are not directly equivalent.

**Assessment:** higher sustainable cash flow, faster growth or a lower required return can support the price. Conservative assumptions do not automatically establish a margin of safety. Terminal value dominates the model; no cell is a precise price target. Establish sustainable shareholder cash flow before choosing a valuation multiple.

## Inversion and subsequent monitoring

### 10. What would weaken the thesis?

Food-safety failures could damage the brand's foundation. Trading down, substitution toward other drinks, and changing birth and infant-formula demand can constrain categories. Rising raw-milk costs alongside retail promotions could compress margins. Channel loading, bill maturities and lower customer advances can reverse cash benefits. Further overseas impairment, currency movements, supply-chain credit losses and short-debt/long-deposit mismatches can weaken shareholder returns. An excessive valuation can produce poor returns despite stable business performance.

Evidence supports brand/distribution scale, positive OCF and the start of actual repurchases. Earnings recovery alongside revenue growth and acquisition returns remain challenged. Genuine sell-through, cash generation excluding payment-timing effects, complete deposit/debt matching and sustainable common-shareholder cash flow remain unproven. Limited quarterly notes do not justify inventing distributor inventory, maintenance capex or pure-dairy ROIC.
